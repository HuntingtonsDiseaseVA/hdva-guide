💊 Medicare & Medicaid for HD Families

Navigating Medicare and Medicaid with Huntington's Disease is complex. This guide covers what you're entitled to, how to get it, and what HD families in Virginia need to know.

💡 HD qualifies for SSDI's Compassionate Allowances program. Once approved, Medicare follows after a 29-month waiting period. Backpay counts, so most HD families wait far less.
How HD Patients Qualify for Medicare
Step 1: Apply for SSDI (HD qualifies under Compassionate Allowances)
Step 2: 5-month SSDI waiting period
Step 3: 24 qualifying SSDI payments — backpay counts, so most wait far less than 29 months
Step 4: Medicare Part A and Part B begin automatically
Medicare Parts A, B, C, D for HD
Part A — Hospital: Inpatient stays, skilled nursing (up to 100 days), hospice, home health. Most pay $0 premium.
Part B — Medical: HD neurologist, outpatient PT/OT/SLP, DME, home health. ~$185/month (2025).
Part C — Medicare Advantage: Bundles A+B+usually D. 143 plans in Virginia 2026. Verify HD neurologist is in-network.
Part D — Prescriptions: Covers tetrabenazine/Austedo, antidepressants, antipsychotics. Starting $0/month in Virginia.
What Medicare Covers for HD
✅ HD neurologist (Part B)   ✅ PT, OT, SLP (Part B)   ✅ Durable medical equipment / AAC devices (Part B)
✅ Home health when homebound (Part A/B)   ✅ Hospice (Part A)   ✅ Skilled nursing up to 100 days (Part A)
✅ Tetrabenazine/Austedo for chorea (Part D)   ✅ Psychiatric medications (Part D)
❌ Long-term custodial care (Medicaid covers this)   ❌ Most dental/vision/hearing

📅 Onset Date — The Most Important Date

SSA starts the Medicare clock from the date you became disabled — not application date, not approval date. Documenting the earliest supportable onset date directly accelerates Medicare eligibility and maximizes back pay.

✅ Medicare clock starts from onset date
✅ Back pay calculated from onset date — earlier onset = more back pay
✅ SSDI pays up to 12 months before application date
⚠️ Wrong onset date can cost thousands in lost benefits
Onset date = when HD symptoms first impacted your ability to work — typically EARLIER than diagnosis date.

Document with: Employer performance records, medical notes from that period, caregiver statements, neurologist letter.

Anosognosia: HD patients underestimate their own limitations. Caregiver accounts are more accurate — include them.

🏛️ SSDI & SSI for HD Families

Social Security disability benefits are often the financial lifeline for HD patients and their families.

💡 HD is on the Compassionate Allowances list (CAL Code: DI 23022.923) — applications are fast-tracked for approval in 2–6 weeks. Always include this code when applying.
SSDI vs SSI — Which Applies to You?

Two separate programs. Many HD families qualify for both. SSA screens every SSDI applicant for SSI automatically.

SSDI
Social Security Disability Insurance
Based on: Work history & FICA taxes
2025 SGA: Must earn <$1,550/month
Leads to: Medicare (29-month wait)
No asset limit
SSI
Supplemental Security Income
Based on: Financial need
2025 max: $967/month individual
Leads to: Medicaid immediately
Asset limit: $2,000 individual
⚠️ SSI can provide income during the SSDI 5-month waiting period. Always apply for both if income and assets permit.
The Compassionate Allowance (CAL) Code — DI 23022.923
Include this exact language in the Remarks section of every SSDI/SSI application:

"Please flag my claim for CAL processing per DI 23022.923 because I have Huntington's Disease."
Approved in 2–6 weeks vs. months without this flag.
You do NOT need a gene test — symptom documentation alone can qualify.

Required evidence: HD gene test (40+ CAG repeats if available), neurologist records, all provider records, employer records showing performance decline.
📄 HDSA Compassionate Allowance Guide →
SSDI Eligibility — 2025
Work Credits: 40 total, 20 earned in last 10 years. In 2025: 1 credit per $1,810 earned (max 4/year).
SGA: Must earn less than $1,550/month gross
Medical: HD qualifies under Compassionate Allowance (CAL Code DI 23022.923)
Duration: Must be disabling for 12+ months — HD is progressive and terminal, never an issue
SSI Eligibility — 2025
Income: Countable income below $967/month
Assets: $2,000 individual / $3,000 couple (ABLE accounts up to $100,000 excluded)
Medical: Same as SSDI — CAL applies
No work history required
Blue Book Listings for HD
Listing 11.17 — Adult HD: Option A: Disorganization of motor function in 2 extremities causing extreme limitation. Option B: Marked limitation in physical functioning AND one of: understanding/memory, social interaction, concentration, or self-management.

Listing 12.02 — Neurocognitive: For HD patients whose primary symptoms are cognitive.

Listing 111.17 — Juvenile HD (under 18): Qualifies for SSI.
How to Apply
1. Apply as soon as possible — the 5-month wait and 29-month Medicare clock start from onset date, not application date.
2. Apply online at ssa.gov/applyfordisability, by phone 1-800-772-1213, or in person.
3. Include the CAL flag: "Please flag my claim for CAL processing per DI 23022.923 because I have Huntington's Disease."
4. Use the earliest onset date your medical records support — not your diagnosis date.
5. Submit complete medical evidence with the application — don't wait for SSA to request it.
6. Apply for SSI simultaneously.
7. Get an SSDI attorney involved early — most work on contingency.
💡 With CAL: 2–6 weeks for decision. Back pay paid as lump sum when approved. SSI has no 5-month waiting period.
Complete Document Checklist
Personal
☐ Social Security card ☐ Birth certificate ☐ W-2/tax returns (last 2 years) ☐ DD-214 if veteran ☐ Bank account for direct deposit
Medical — Most Critical
☐ HD gene test results (40+ CAG repeats — strongest evidence)
☐ Neurologist records documenting HD symptoms and functional limitations
☐ Brain imaging (MRI/CT) if performed
☐ Psychiatrist/psychologist records
☐ PT, OT, Speech therapy records
☐ Neurologist letter stating onset date and functional limitations — request this specifically
☐ All PCP, hospital, and specialist records
Work & Function
☐ Work history for last 15 years
☐ Employer records showing performance decline — reviews, accommodation letters
☐ Third-party function report (SSA Form SSA-787) — completed by caregiver
⚠️ Anosognosia: HD patients underreport limitations. Have caregivers help complete function reports and write separate third-party statements.
If Denied — Don't Give Up

Most initial SSDI applications are denied — even with HD. You have 60 days from the denial date to appeal.

Level 1 — Reconsideration: Different examiner reviews. File within 60 days.
Level 2 — Administrative Law Judge (ALJ) Hearing: Highest approval rate. Get an attorney before this.
Level 3 — Appeals Council
Level 4 — Federal District Court
💡 Attorneys work on contingency — 25% of back pay (capped at $7,200) only if you win. No upfront cost.

🎖️ Veterans with HD — Navigation Guide

Navigating the VA system with Huntington's Disease is one of the hardest things a veteran or family will ever do. This guide is written from real experience — not from a brochure. The system will not come to you. You have to drive every single step.

⚠️ A note on navigating the system: VA social workers often carry heavy caseloads and may not be familiar with HD-specific programs and needs. You may need to be your own advocate — follow up on every request, keep notes of every conversation, and don't wait for the next step to come to you.
⚠️ Veterans programs have specific eligibility requirements. Being a veteran with HD does not automatically qualify you for all VA programs.
⚠️ Before You Start — Read This
• Enroll in VA healthcare even if you have Tricare. They are completely separate systems with different benefits. Enroll in both.
• Social workers are assigned — but don't count on them. You may need to be your own case manager. Follow up on every request in writing.
• Every benefit is a separate application. Nothing automatically flows to the next thing.
• The VA will default to the Parkinson's Clinic for movement disorders. Say: "I need a neurologist experienced in Huntington's Disease specifically."
• Community Care referrals must be ordered by your doctor — not you, not the social worker.
• Watch for letters with 30-day response deadlines. Missing them can cost benefits.
• Start every application before you desperately need it. Veteran Directed Care (VDC) has a 6–8 week lead time. Program of Comprehensive Assistance for Family Caregivers (PCAFC) has weeks of intake. SSDI will likely be denied the first time.
Step 1 — Enroll in VA Healthcare

This is the first gate. Nothing else is accessible until you are enrolled. Even with Tricare or private insurance, enroll separately — it unlocks Parkinson's Disease Research, Education and Clinical Center (PADRECC), home programs, caregiver benefits, and housing grants.

🌐 Apply online — Form 10-10EZ →
📞 Or call: 1-877-222-8387
💡 Your Primary Care Manager (PCM) becomes your gatekeeper for all referrals. They can order Community Care referrals, assist with Aid and Attendance (A&A) applications, and complete Activities of Daily Living (ADL) assessments as needs change.
Step 2 — Meet Your VA Social Worker and Primary Care Manager (PCM) — Then Don't Wait

Request a meeting immediately. Give them a written list of everything you need. Follow up in writing every week. Keep a log of every call — date, who you spoke with, what was said, what the next step is.

⚠️ Real talk: Social workers are often overloaded and may not proactively advocate for you. The veterans and families who get the most benefits are persistent, organized, and follow up in writing.
Step 3 — Get HD-Specific Neurological Care (Not the Parkinson's Clinic)
💬 Say exactly this: "I need a referral to a neurologist with experience in Huntington's Disease specifically — not just general movement disorders. Please refer me through Community Care to an HD specialist."
📞 PADRECC Richmond: (804) 675-5000 — HD specialty hub for Virginia veterans
🌐 parkinsons.va.gov/richmond →
Step 4 — File for VA Disability Rating Immediately
⚠️ Real experience: An initial rating of 30% for HD was successfully appealed to 100%. The key was documenting loss of use of hands and feet, gait disturbances, and motor control loss due to chorea — all framed as Activities of Daily Living (ADL) impact.
🌐 File a claim — Form 21-526EZ →
📞 Virginia Department of Veterans Services (VDVS): (804) 675-6546
Step 5 — Appeal for 100% Permanent and Total (P&T)

HD is terminal and progressive. A rating below 100% should almost always be appealed. 100% P&T unlocks Specially Adapted Housing (SAH) grant, vehicle grant, Civilian Health and Medical Program of the Department of Veterans Affairs (CHAMPVA), Dependents' Educational Assistance (DEA), free dental, and Priority Group 1 health care.

• 100% Schedular — conditions combine to 100%
• Total Disability Individual Unemployability (TDIU) — paid at 100% if HD prevents all employment. File VA Form 21-8940.
Step 6 — Apply for SSDI Simultaneously

VA rating and SSDI are completely separate systems. Apply for both at the same time.

⚠️ Real experience: SSDI was denied on the first application. Approved after appeal. Get an attorney early. Always include: "Please flag my claim for CAL processing per DI 23022.923 because I have Huntington's Disease."
📞 SSA: 1-800-772-1213 | 📞 Virginia DDS: (804) 367-4707
Step 7 — Activate Home & Caregiver Programs
☐ Veteran Directed Care (VDC) — 6–8 week lead time. Your Primary Care Manager (PCM) must order a consult.
☐ Program of Comprehensive Assistance for Family Caregivers (PCAFC) — Weeks of intake. Form 10-10CG →
☐ Aid and Attendance (A&A) — Your Primary Care Manager (PCM) or VA nurse can complete VA Form 21-2680.
☐ Housing grants — after 100% rating is established.

💡 You can be enrolled in both Veteran Directed Care (VDC) and Program of Comprehensive Assistance for Family Caregivers (PCAFC) simultaneously if needs are documented and high enough.
VA Disability Rating — What You Need to Know
⚠️ Real experience: Initial rating of 30% for HD was successfully appealed to 100% by documenting loss of use of hands and feet, gait disturbances, and motor control loss due to chorea — all articulated as Activities of Daily Living (ADL) impact.
Two paths to 100%:
• 100% Schedular: Individual or combined conditions reach 100%
• Total Disability Individual Unemployability (TDIU): Paid at 100% if HD prevents all gainful employment — VA Form 21-8940

✅ Specially Adapted Housing (SAH) grant (up to $126,000)

How to Appeal a Low Rating
1. Request Rating Decision letter and Compensation & Pension (C&P) exam results
2. Get a private nexus letter from your neurologist documenting functional limitations in ADL language
3. File Supplemental Claim (new evidence) or Higher-Level Review
4. If still denied → Board of Veterans Appeals

Key appeal language for HD:
• Loss of use of extremities due to chorea and motor control loss
• Fall risk and gait instability — inability to walk without assistance
• Fine motor loss — cannot write, type, button clothing, use utensils
• Cognitive impairment affecting finances, medications, daily tasks
• Psychiatric symptoms — depression, irritability, behavioral dyscontrol

📋 Activities of Daily Living (ADL) Rating — The Most Important Documentation Strategy

The VA rating system responds to functional language — specifically how symptoms affect Activities of Daily Living (ADLs). Every symptom must be translated into what the veteran cannot do.

💡 "Chorea" means little to a VA rater. "Loss of use of hands — unable to button clothing, hold utensils, or write" means everything.
HD Symptoms → Activities of Daily Living (ADL) Language
Motor → ADL:
Chorea → "Loss of use of hands — cannot grip utensils, button clothing, write, or handle objects safely"
Gait disturbance → "Loss of use of feet — cannot walk without fall risk, cannot climb stairs"
Balance → "Cannot stand without support — fall risk requires supervision for all standing activities"
Swallowing → "Cannot eat independently without choking risk"

Cognitive → ADL:
Executive dysfunction → "Cannot manage finances, medications, or daily scheduling without assistance"
Memory → "Cannot follow multi-step instructions or remember recent events"

Psychiatric → ADL:
Irritability → "Cannot interact appropriately in community or workplace settings"
Apathy → "Cannot initiate daily activities without prompting"
Who Can Document Activities of Daily Living (ADLs)
✅ Your VA Primary Care Manager (PCM) or Nurse — can complete ADL assessments as needs change. Also assists with Aid and Attendance (A&A) applications (VA Form 21-2680).
✅ Occupational Therapist (OT) — most qualified for ADL documentation
✅ Caregiver statements — third-party accounts carry significant weight
✅ HD Neurologist — should document functional limitations in every clinic note

Anosognosia note: HD patients often underestimate their own limitations. Caregiver accounts are often more accurate. Mention this to your VA team.
📊 Special Monthly Compensation (SMC) — The Rating Scale HD Veterans Should Know

Service Connection for HD — The Stress Aggravation Argument

The VA's default position is that HD is a genetic condition that existed before service and cannot be service-connected. This is not the complete legal or medical picture.

The science: CAG repeat length explains only ~60% of variation in HD onset age. Chronic stress is a documented modifier of HD onset and progression — published research demonstrates that stress accelerates memory deficits and exacerbates cellular dysfunction in HD. Military service is among the most chronically stressful environments humans experience.
Legal pathways:
1. Service Aggravation (38 CFR 3.306) — If symptoms appeared earlier than expected given the veteran's CAG repeat length, military stress may have accelerated onset beyond natural progression.
2. Presumption of Soundness — If HD was not noted on the entrance physical, VA must prove with "clear and unmistakable evidence" that HD both pre-existed service AND was not aggravated by service.
3. Earlier-Than-Expected Onset — The gap between predicted and actual onset age based on CAG repeat count is the aggravation argument.
4. Eight-Year Rule — Veterans with 8+ years of active duty have additional pathways for hereditary conditions manifesting during service.
⚠️ VA examiners often write that "HD cannot be aggravated" — this is scientifically contested and legally challengeable. Work with a Veteran Service Organization (VSO) or veterans attorney. Do not accept the first denial.

🌐 National Conferences & Events
HDSA Convention (Annual, Summer)

The largest annual gathering of the HD community in the United States. Research updates, clinical presentations, caregiver workshops, peer support, and advocacy sessions. Open to patients, families, researchers, and clinicians. Financial assistance available through HDSA.

🌐 hdsa.org/convention →

HD Therapeutics Conference — HDSA (Annual, February)

Annual scientific conference focused on HD drug development and research pipeline. Attended by researchers, clinicians, and industry. Patient and family sessions included. Livestream often available.

May is HD Awareness Month. International HD Awareness Day is May 15. Events, walks, and community gatherings happen throughout the month across Virginia and the country. Use #LetsTalkAboutHD on social media to share and connect.
